============================================================
EXPERT ROUNDUP PROMPT
============================================================

Covers: expert-quotes article (10+ named practitioners answering one focused
question) + the outreach email + the contribution brief + the final
attribution / promotion pack. Built for link-earning and citation authority.

BRAND DETECTION: Follow _Brand_Detection_Rules.txt before generating.

Once the brand is confirmed, use the template below.

------------------------------------------------------------
INPUTS REQUIRED
------------------------------------------------------------

- BRAND: [BRAND NAME], [BRAND DESCRIPTION]
- ROUNDUP QUESTION: the single focused question every expert will answer (must be narrow enough that a specific expert with a specific view can answer it in 100-200 words). Bad: "How is AI changing marketing?" Good: "What is the one thing your team stopped doing in 2026 because AI made it obsolete?"
- TARGET AUDIENCE: [AUDIENCE - who reads this]
- TARGET EXPERT COUNT: 10-30 (default 15)
- EXPERT SEGMENT: named category(-ies) of practitioner (e.g. "Head of Growth at Series B B2B SaaS in the US and UK", "DTC founders under 5M ARR", "creator-economy operators")
- PUBLISH DATE (target): [YYYY-MM-DD]

If the ROUNDUP QUESTION is broad or off-brand, propose 2-3 sharper alternatives and stop.

------------------------------------------------------------
DELIVERABLE 1 - THE OUTREACH EMAIL (to prospective contributors)
------------------------------------------------------------

Rules:
- 100-140 words. Any longer and busy experts do not reply.
- Personalisation slot at the top: `[Recipient-specific line - reference their recent work by name and year, not "I love your content"]`. If personalisation is impossible for a batch, do NOT send - a bulk-templated expert email tanks reply rate.
- Name the SPECIFIC question up front. Reader must know in the first 30 words what you are asking of them.
- State the concrete ask: how many words (100-200), by when (specific date, at least 2 weeks out), and where it will publish (blog URL + estimated publish date + audience size context).
- State what they get in return: a named-link back to their profile / company, a heads-up when it goes live, permission to reuse their quote in their own content.
- No banned CTA phrases ("Learn more", "Discover"). CTA is a single question: "Are you in?" or "Would you have 15 minutes to answer this by [date]?"
- Sign off with a real named human, not a marketing alias.

Output the email as ready-to-send text plus a suggested subject line (under 55 chars, plain, no ALL CAPS, no emoji unless the audience is B2C).

------------------------------------------------------------
DELIVERABLE 2 - THE CONTRIBUTION BRIEF (sent to experts who say yes)
------------------------------------------------------------

One-page brief they can act on without a second email:
- THE QUESTION (verbatim, front and centre)
- WORD COUNT: 100-200 words
- ANGLE ASK: "Answer from your own direct experience, not general theory. One specific example beats three general points. First-person voice is fine and encouraged."
- BANNED (in their submission): fabricated stats, generic frameworks, unattributed claims. If they cite a stat, they must name the source and year.
- FORMAT: plain text or Google Doc, no formatting required
- SUBMISSION: email their response to [address] by [deadline]
- ATTRIBUTION FORMAT: "Name, Title at Company - [linked to profile URL of their choice: LinkedIn / personal site / X]"
- HEADSHOT REQUEST: 400x400 minimum, .jpg or .png, transparent-background version if available
- RIGHTS: brand can use the quote in the article + one social pull-quote per contributor; contributor keeps all other rights
- HEADS-UP PROMISE: brand will email the day the article goes live + tag them in the launch social post

------------------------------------------------------------
DELIVERABLE 3 - THE ARTICLE STRUCTURE
------------------------------------------------------------

- H1: names the question, promises the number of experts ("15 growth leads on the one thing they stopped doing in 2026")
- INTRO (120-180 words): frames why this question matters right now, in the target audience's vocabulary. Ends with "Here is what N practitioners told us."
- STRUCTURING PATTERN: choose ONE based on the answers:
    * If answers cluster: group by theme (3-5 themes) with 3-5 expert quotes per theme + a section intro tying them together.
    * If answers diverge: one quote per section, ordered by strongest-claim-first, most-contrarian second, safest-consensus last (order matters for time-on-page).
- EACH EXPERT QUOTE:
    * Attribution line: Name, Title at Company (with the link the expert supplied)
    * Headshot at consistent size on the left
    * Quote body (100-200 words, lightly edited for clarity only - never re-written)
    * Optional 1-line editor's note beneath if the quote references something worth linking or explaining
- CONCLUSION (150-200 words): synthesise across the answers WITHOUT flattening them. Name the tension between the divergent views if there is one; do not fake a consensus.
- METHODOLOGY NOTE (mandatory footer): who was asked, how many replied, publication date, and whether any responses were edited for length.

------------------------------------------------------------
DELIVERABLE 4 - PROMOTION PACK
------------------------------------------------------------

- SOCIAL PULL-QUOTES: one per contributor, ready to schedule, with the contributor tagged and the article URL. Format for LinkedIn (200-400 chars) and X (under 260 chars).
- CONTRIBUTOR-HEADS-UP EMAIL: sent the day of publish - short thank-you + link + the tagged social posts (asking, not requiring, a reshare).
- INTERNAL DISTRIBUTION: 1-line internal Slack post + 1-line for the sales team + 1-line for CS.

------------------------------------------------------------
FAILURE MODES TO AVOID
------------------------------------------------------------

1. QUESTION TOO BROAD: "How is X changing?" gets vague answers and looks like a listicle. Must be narrow enough to answer specifically.
2. TEMPLATED OUTREACH: a form email to 500 experts gets a 2% reply rate. Personalise or do not send.
3. RE-WRITING CONTRIBUTOR QUOTES: light editing for clarity only. If a quote fundamentally changes meaning, ask the contributor - never silently rewrite.
4. FABRICATED CONTRIBUTORS: if you fall short of the target count, publish with what you have and update later; NEVER invent an expert or attribute a quote to someone who did not send one.
5. NO METHODOLOGY NOTE: a roundup with no "who was asked / how many replied" reads as PR, not research.
6. TAKING CREDIT FOR THEIR IDEAS IN THE CONCLUSION: synthesise, don't rebrand. Do not put the sharpest contributor line in the intro as if it were the brand's own thesis.

------------------------------------------------------------
SELF-CHECK BEFORE OUTPUT
------------------------------------------------------------

- Outreach email under 140 words with real personalisation slot?
- Brief lists word count, deadline, format, attribution format, rights, and heads-up promise?
- Article structure follows either cluster-by-theme OR order-by-tension?
- Each expert has attribution + headshot + linked profile URL slot?
- Methodology note present at the bottom?
- Promotion pack tags contributors, does not compel a reshare?
- No banned CTA phrases, no em dashes?

------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT FORMAT
------------------------------------------------------------

Return, in order:
1. OUTREACH EMAIL (with subject line)
2. CONTRIBUTION BRIEF
3. ARTICLE STRUCTURE (with placeholders for the quotes)
4. PROMOTION PACK
